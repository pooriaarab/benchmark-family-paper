Probing review found defects in every package after the builders' gates and the council's reviews had run. This section reports what broke; Appendix~\ref{app:reviewstats} holds the review-record statistics and Appendix~\ref{app:builders} the builder record. Every count below is a sourced minimum drawn from a public changelog, issue, grading note, or pull-request thread; Table~\ref{tab:defects} names the source per class, Appendix~\ref{app:locators} lists the locator behind every package cell, and Figure~\ref{fig:defects} charts the counts.

\paragraph{Probing review.}
The probing sessions ran separate from the builders, instructed to distrust the builders' claims, write their own attack policies, and verify against running code. The author configured each of those sessions and handed it a protocol; the reviewers were agent sessions, so this paper calls the pass a probing review, and the protocol the first-round sessions followed is not released. The released protocol in Appendix~\ref{app:protocol} is the re-review one, which the re-check sessions used after fixes landed. Two things about these sessions are not verifiable on the public record: authorship, because the sessions ran in coding-agent interfaces and the model behind them is not recorded in the repositories, and coverage, because a defect class nobody thought to probe would not appear. What the record does show is the output: findings, fixes, and locators that anyone can check again.

\paragraph{The taxonomy.}
Table~\ref{tab:defects} sorts the findings into \defectClasses{} classes, and Appendix~\ref{app:defectclasses} walks each class with its examples and sourced minima. The classes that move a grader score cover \defectScoreMaterial{} of the \reviewPackages{} packages; \defectNoClassPackages{} needed its fix rounds without a defect entering a sourced class. The widest are strategies that ignore the task and still earn credit (at least \defectBlindStrategyCredit{} packages), honest answers scored zero over formatting alone (at least \defectHonestAnswerRejected{} packages), and hidden-split handling that was not hidden. The rest cover unsolvable tasks, unpunished violations, the SDK task-list cache, breakdowns the engine dropped, forged report rows, an evaluator a crafted answer could escape, documentation ahead of the code, generator bugs, lexical detectors that miss unseen wording, and prompts looser than the grader. The suite shipped in two waves: the \reviewWaveOne{} earlier packages form wave 1, and the \expWaveTwo{} later ones, with only the 0.1.0 release so far, form wave 2. All \reviewPackages{} packages needed at least one fix round before or after release, with a median of \reviewRoundsMedian{} rounds and a maximum of \reviewRoundsMax{} (data in \texttt{paper/data/review-rounds.csv}). Every wave-1 package changed its hidden-split handling at 0.2.0, and each shipped a changelog that states 0.1.0 scores are not comparable; the fix briefs, the written findings a review round hands the builder, required that sentence, and the scoring changes behind it are real and on the same record.

\paragraph{The re-measurement.}
The method in one sentence: the frozen exploit list, \expExplFrozen{} rows in \texttt{paper/data/exp/exploits.csv} written before the runs, gives each logged exploit one or more clauses, where a clause is a single checkable relation, a record clause checks the logged number on a named version or commit, and a post-fix clause checks the shipped fix against a stated relation; r$n$ below cites a row of that list. The re-measurement backs the record on the numbers it could check: \expExplReplicated{} of the \expExplRecordClauses{} clauses that re-measure a logged number or bound reproduce it, \expExplNotReplicated{} do not, and \expExplFixHolds{} of the \expExplPostfixClauses{} post-fix clauses hold, \expExplFixHoldsWeak{} of them only against the loose \emph{below 0.1.0} bound, the weakest relation the list states, which asks that the new mean sit under the old one with no margin. The runs evaluated \expExplClauses{} clauses over \expExplRunRows{} of the rows: \expExplReleasedRows{} on the released artifacts, \expExplCommitRows{} on the pinned intermediate commits the record cited, unreleased source states, and the other \expExplPolicyOnly{} need the reviewer's unreleased policy. Of the replications \expExplReplCommit{} sit on those unreleased commits, as do \expExplNotReplCommit{} of the misses; the misses, measured value against the record, are \expExplNotReplicatedList{}. The \expExplFixFails{} post-fix clauses that fail split two ways: the shipped 0.2.0 still pays the exploit on \expExplFixFailsAttackPkgs{} (checkout-consent's stall traces still earn full credit on part of the task set; the tool-errors write-wait-write still lifts a task over an empty submission), and it still rejects an honest answer on \expExplFixFailsHonestPkgs{} (pelican's entity-escaped reference SVG, marked \emph{honest} in Table~\ref{tab:exploit-verdicts}). On the \expExplAtkPairs{} wave-1 attack rows measured at both versions the median score falls from \expExplAtkMedianOldPaired{} at 0.1.0 to \expExplAtkMedianNewPaired{} at 0.2.0, and the largest post-fix attack mean is \expExplAtkMaxNew{}, hundred-step's stop-one-step-early trace, where completed steps keep legitimate partial credit; \expExplAtkNewOnly{} wave-1 attack row carries only a 0.2.0 clause and stays outside the pair. Figure~\ref{fig:exploits} plots the record against the re-measurement for every frozen row the tarball runs measured; the wave-2 rows below the rule have only a 0.1.0 release, some already carrying review fixes, so they are exploratory and sit outside the before/after claim. Because the exploit list is post hoc, the run confirms the logged numbers and the fixes behind them on the artifacts it checked; it says nothing about how often probing misses a defect.

\paragraph{The review record, for and against.}
The record documents \approvalDefectCases{} approvals standing behind defects the probing review found later: pelican's pull request 4, issue 8, and sycophancy's pull request 20, approved while a publishing leak and a grading-note claim the code did not back were still in the tree. The counter-evidence is real too: the council approved \councilMergedApproved{} of the \councilMergedFeature{} merged feature pull requests and its reviews raised an open Critical or Major item on about \councilSeverityPrsRead{} of them, and one defect in Table~\ref{tab:defects}, the published hidden seed on sycophancy's pull request 20, was flagged by the council itself before merge.

\paragraph{What the record does and does not show.}
One caveat sits over the whole section: the defect table is built from the probing record, so ``probing found them'' is partly true by construction. The parts that do not rest on the record are the re-measurements: the exploit clause verdicts above, the trivial battery in Section~\ref{sec:prereg}, and the contract probes in Section~\ref{sec:soundness}. Not every finding closed: crm-truthfulness ships as alpha with its lexical miss rate documented as a known limit, and pelican's structural check accepts drawings a person would reject and rejects some it would accept, a documented limit that is still open. A published mean that does not name its package version is also not reproducible, and Table~\ref{tab:suite} carries the versions for that reason.
