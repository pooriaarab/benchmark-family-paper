Probing review found defects in every package after the builders' gates and the council's reviews had run. This section reports what broke; Appendix~\ref{app:reviewstats} holds the review-record statistics and Appendix~\ref{app:builders} the builder record. Every count below is a sourced minimum drawn from a public changelog, issue, grading note, or pull-request thread; Table~\ref{tab:defects} names the source per class, Appendix~\ref{app:locators} lists the locator behind every package cell, and Figure~\ref{fig:defects} charts the counts.

\paragraph{Probing review.}
The probing sessions ran separate from the builders, instructed to distrust the builders' claims, write their own attack policies, and verify against running code. Appendix~\ref{app:protocol} releases the re-review protocol the re-check sessions used after fixes landed; the first-round protocol is not released. The coverage the record cannot show is the part nobody probed: a defect class nobody thought to check would not appear. What it does show is the output: findings, fixes, and locators that anyone can check again.

\paragraph{The taxonomy.}
Table~\ref{tab:defects} sorts the findings into \defectClasses{} classes, and Appendix~\ref{app:defectclasses} walks each class with its examples and sourced minima. The classes that move a grader score cover \defectScoreMaterial{} of the \reviewPackages{} packages; \defectNoClassPackages{} needed its fix rounds without a defect entering a sourced class. The widest are strategies that ignore the task and still earn credit (at least \defectBlindStrategyCredit{} packages), honest answers scored zero over formatting alone (at least \defectHonestAnswerRejected{} packages), and hidden-split handling that was not hidden. The rest cover unsolvable tasks, unpunished violations, the SDK task-list cache, breakdowns the engine dropped, forged report rows, an evaluator a crafted answer could escape, documentation ahead of the code, generator bugs, lexical detectors that miss unseen wording, and prompts looser than the grader. The suite shipped in waves: the \reviewWaveOne{} earlier packages form wave 1, and the \expWaveTwo{} later ones, with only the 0.1.0 release so far, form wave 2. All \reviewPackages{} packages needed at least one fix round before or after release, with a median of \reviewRoundsMedian{} rounds and a maximum of \reviewRoundsMax{}. Every wave-1 package changed its hidden-split handling at 0.2.0, and each shipped a changelog that states 0.1.0 scores are not comparable; the scoring changes behind it are real and on the same record.

\paragraph{The re-measurement.}
The method in one sentence: a frozen exploit list of \expExplFrozen{} rows written before the runs gives each logged exploit one or more clauses, where a clause is a single checkable relation, a record clause checks the logged number on a named version or commit, and a post-fix clause checks the shipped fix against a stated relation; Appendix~\ref{app:experiments} carries the row bookkeeping and Table~\ref{tab:exploit-verdicts} in Appendix~\ref{app:results} the per-part counts. The re-measurement backs the record on the numbers it could check: \expExplReplicated{} of the \expExplRecordClauses{} record clauses replicate (\expExplReplReleased{} of \expExplRecordReleased{} on the released artifacts, \expExplReplCommit{} of \expExplRecordCommit{} on the pinned commits the record cited), \expExplNotReplicated{} do not, and \expExplFixHolds{} of the \expExplPostfixClauses{} post-fix clauses hold, \expExplFixHoldsWeak{} of them only under the weak lower-than-before bound that asks the new mean to sit under the old one with no margin; the misses sit on \expExplNotReplicatedPkgs{}. The \expExplFixFails{} post-fix clauses that fail split two ways: the shipped 0.2.0 still pays the exploit on \expExplFixFailsAttackPkgs{} (checkout-consent's stall traces still earn full credit on part of the task set; the tool-errors write-wait-write still lifts a task over an empty submission), and it still rejects an honest answer on \expExplFixFailsHonestPkgs{} (pelican's entity-escaped reference SVG, marked \emph{honest} in Table~\ref{tab:exploit-verdicts}). The weakest hold worth a number is \texttt{mcp}-\allowbreak\texttt{safety}'s r21 attack, which still earns \expExplWeakMcpSafety{} at 0.2.0; the re-measurement reads that residual as the product rule paying the runs its own safety leg marks safe and useful, with the same leg rejecting the blind call list wherever it trips a trap, so the clause holds only under the weak bound rather than under a real score bound. On the \expExplAtkPairs{} wave-1 attack rows measured at both versions the median score falls from \expExplAtkMedianOldPaired{} at 0.1.0 to \expExplAtkMedianNewPaired{} at 0.2.0; Figure~\ref{fig:exploits} in the appendix plots the record against the re-measurement for every frozen row the tarball runs measured. Because the exploit list is post hoc, the run confirms the logged numbers and the fixes behind them on the artifacts it checked; it says nothing about how often probing misses a defect.

\paragraph{The review record, for and against.}
The record documents \approvalDefectCases{} approvals standing behind defects the probing review found later; Appendix~\ref{app:reviewstats} lists both cases with their locators. The record also runs against the headline: the council approved most merged feature pull requests and its reviews still raised an open Critical or Major item on \councilSeverityFeaturePrs{} of them, and one defect in Table~\ref{tab:defects}, the published hidden seed on sycophancy, was flagged by the council itself before merge.

\paragraph{What the record does and does not show.}
One caveat sits over the whole section: the defect table is built from the probing record, so the counts in it are partly by construction; Section~\ref{sec:limits} carries the full statement and names the parts that do not rest on the record. Not every finding closed: crm-truthfulness ships as alpha with its lexical miss rate documented as a known limit, and pelican's structural check accepts drawings a person would reject and rejects some it would accept, a documented limit that is still open. A published mean that does not name its package version is also not reproducible, and Table~\ref{tab:suite} carries the versions for that reason.
