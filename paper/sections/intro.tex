Coding agents built the \suitePackages{} benchmark packages in this suite, \suiteTasks{} tasks on one shared engine, and every package passed its tests while most of the \councilMergedFeature{} merged feature pull requests carried an LLM approval. Probing the running packages still found defects that needed a fix round in every one of them; the \suitePackages{} cases the title counts are the packages, and the record documents \approvalDefectCases{} of the \councilMergedApproved{} approvals as approval-to-defect cases.

Benchmarks steer model choice, safety claims and research agendas, and a score is only as good as the grader behind it. Coding agents have made a benchmark cheap to build: a task set, a deterministic grader and runnable surfaces in days. Verification has not become cheap. The usual gates are unit tests, continuous integration and a reviewer reading the diff, and the published checklists add an oracle solver and a trivial agent~\cite{reuel2024betterbench,zhu2025abc}. This paper measures what those gates miss on \suitePackages{} real packages, and what a deeper review finds.

The record behind every number is public. All \reviewPackages{} packages needed at least one fix round (median \reviewRoundsMedian{}, maximum \reviewRoundsMax{}) across \defectClasses{} defect classes, \expExplReplicated{} of \expExplRecordClauses{} logged exploit clauses replicate on re-measurement, \expExplReplReleased{} of \expExplRecordReleased{} on the published tarballs and \expExplReplCommit{} of \expExplRecordCommit{} on the unreleased commits the record cited, the median attack score over the \expExplAtkPairs{} paired wave-1 exploit rows falls from \expExplAtkMedianOldPaired{} before the fixes to \expExplAtkMedianNewPaired{} after, and under the preregistered design the best generic blind policy scored higher before the fixes in only \trivialHigherOld{} of \trivialPackages{} packages ($p = \trivialSignP{}$ on \trivialSignN{} non-tied packages). The record also runs against the headline: the workflow raised open Critical or Major items on about \councilSeverityPrsRead{} of the \councilPrsReviewed{} pull requests it reviewed, the diff-reading review flagged a published hidden seed before one merge, and the generic blind battery found released credit on \expTrivialReleasedMaxPkgs{} that the record never logged. There is no human-built control and no real model has been run, so this paper claims nothing about any model's ability.
