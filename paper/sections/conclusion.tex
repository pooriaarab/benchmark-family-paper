The point of view of this paper is narrow: coding agents produce working benchmark code quickly, and the defects that matter are the ones their own tests were never written to find. On this suite, a builder's green checks predicted nothing about whether its grader could be gamed, its tasks solved, or its hidden split kept hidden; an independent pass found defects in all \suitePackages{} packages. That is a claim about builders reviewing their own work. It is not a claim that agents cannot review.

The defect classes in Table~\ref{tab:defects} compress into five checks we now require before any benchmark in this family ships, and we offer them to anyone publishing an agent-built benchmark:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Solvable from the model's view.} A policy that reads only what a real model sees must pass every task kind. Unsolvable tasks and prompts looser than their grader fail this check.
\item \textbf{Blind policies lose.} Constant, empty, always-yes, always-refuse, repeated-call, and task-ignoring strategies must score near the floor. Blind-strategy credit was the most common defect in this suite.
\item \textbf{Honest answers win.} A correct answer that is differently worded, fenced, or prose-wrapped must score high. Formatting traps and lexical detectors that miss paraphrase fail this check.
\item \textbf{Held out means held out.} A hidden split must differ from the public split in prompts and answers, keep its own ids, stay out of the published package, and never leak the seed that regenerates it.
\item \textbf{Nothing is taken on trust.} Submitted scores, states, and tool results are re-derived by the grader, documentation claims only checks that exist in code, and model-written text never executes in the grader's own process.
\end{enumerate}

None of the five needs a new model or a bigger dataset. All five failed somewhere in this suite while the builder was also the reviewer. Results on real models are future work; the engine, the packages, the protocol, and the defect data are public for anyone who wants to run one.
