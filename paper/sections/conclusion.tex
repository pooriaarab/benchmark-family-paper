A benchmark can pass every gate on the way in and still ship a broken grader. On this suite, green checks and diff-reading approvals left defects in the shipped packages that only probing the running artifacts found. The defect classes compress into five checks we now require before any benchmark in this family ships, and we offer them to anyone releasing an agent-built benchmark:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Solvable from the model's view.} A policy that reads only what a real model sees must pass every task kind. Unsolvable tasks and prompts looser than their grader fail this check.
\item \textbf{Blind policies lose.} Constant, empty, always-yes, always-refuse, repeated-call, and task-ignoring strategies must score near the floor. Blind-strategy credit was the most common defect in this suite. The preregistered check suggests generic policies alone do not cover it: the package still needs attack policies written against its own task shape (Section~\ref{sec:prereg}).
\item \textbf{Honest answers win.} A correct answer that is differently worded, fenced, or prose-wrapped must score high. Formatting traps and lexical detectors that miss paraphrase fail this check.
\item \textbf{Held out means held out.} A hidden split must differ from the public split in prompts and answers, keep its own ids, stay out of the published package, and never leak the seed that regenerates it.
\item \textbf{Nothing is taken on trust.} Submitted scores, states, and tool results are re-derived by the grader, documentation claims only checks that exist in code, and model-written text never executes in the grader's own process.
\end{enumerate}

Three of the checks already run as scripts against this suite and stay cheap: the blind-policy battery of Section~\ref{sec:prereg} covers the second check across the \expArtifacts{} pinned artifacts in \expRuntimeBatteryMin{} minutes, and the frozen contract probes of Section~\ref{sec:soundness} cover the third and fifth in \expRuntimeProbesSec{} seconds; the tarball and held-out-split audits of the same section measure the fourth. Before releasing an agent-built benchmark, exercise the artifact the way a probing review does: freeze an attack list in public, play blind and honest-variant policies against the shipped package, audit what the tarball holds, and write down what each check cannot see.

On this suite a probing pass found defects that green checks and approvals had left in the shipped packages; that is evidence about what each kind of review catches, and it does not show that no defect remains. Results on real models are future work; the engine, the packages, the protocol, and the defect data are public for anyone who wants to run one.
